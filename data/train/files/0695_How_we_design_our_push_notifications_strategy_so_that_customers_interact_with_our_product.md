How we design our push notifications strategy so that customers interact with our product
Generate attractive push notifications detecting opportunities within the behavior of our users, trying to communicate without being spam.
As app users, we are constantly receiving notifications from apps. While some of them may be interesting for us, the fact that we are so used to them can lead us to overlook the important ones, and in this way ignore something that can add value to our lives, such as financing the payment of instalments for something we need. The more applications we use, the more services they try to offer us, which can become a real burden.
With the clear aim of reaching our users effectively and without becoming a nuisance to them, in this article we share our expertise in the development of a process that will allow us to generate and shortlist the candidates that will receive notifications as well as the best alternative for each. We also describe some of the strategies that were developed in this environment. All this is comprised within the Marketplace In Store project, which refers to the catalog of stores that operate with Mercado Pago QR as a means of payment plus the discounts and benefits they offer.
The development was carried out within our Machine Learning platform called Fury Data Apps, using Python as the coding language.
Creating a good product is not enough; it is also necessary to make it known so that it awakens interest within the community, making its use recurring and habitual. On the one hand, boosting the tool promotes users’ awareness of its potential benefits, and on the other hand, it allows greater acceptance by sellers. If the product is well-known and useful to users, it will attract more and better offers which in turn enhance the product. It’s a virtuous circle.. Yet, wrapped in the enthusiasm it entails to announce new functionality and even more so when its related to such a far-reaching tool such as notifications it is possible to make the mistake of overestimating the number of mailings required and failing to be precise when selecting the candidates to be notified.
In this context, , there were already ways of communicating with users, but these communications were ad-hoc and not related to one another. This last condition can lead to the saturation of the channel, since automated mailings are sent without interpreting the relationship across the different strategies. By strategy we mean those policies that make up a contact proposal, such as a proposal based on the user’s interests or another one oriented to certain market niches. That is to say, each strategy reflects a different idea for attracting the user. Although the proposals may be interesting, not all people are the same and perhaps there is a strategy in our catalog most suitable to each.
As we evolved, so did our need to come up with something on a larger scale. Thus what we suggested was to set up a process that would take care of the orchestration, selecting both the best contact for the user in terms of strategy, as well as the best strategy for them among all the possible options. For instance, if one of our strategies is to activate the use in Pet Shops and another one in Hardware stores, we will try to choose the best option within the possible Pet shops and the possible Hardware stores. Finally, we will choose the best candidate between both strategies.
Given the dynamics of the business, there are several initiatives aimed at capturing the user’s attention which are aligned with the market and the final goals. This explains why the structure generated must adapt to user needs at each single moment. To reach this goal, we’ve made an abstraction of the main components of the process so that it is scalable to all possible scenarios. The general components of the process are described below as well as some of the strategies that were implemented on this structure.
Structure of the Process
In order to implement a solution as dynamic as the one described earlier, we’ve had to devise certain modules in such a way that they could be combined.
The initial component of the process is to define what is going to be notified; in our case, stores that collect payments via QR. Out of these stores, it is necessary to define the group the notification is addressed to: stores of a specific category, stores that offer a certain benefit among other possible selections, etc.. We refer to this component as Target.
Then, for each member of the Target, it is necessary to define possible prospects — in our case, all users that for some reason, whether proximity, interests or other conditions, would lead us to think that there could be a match between pairs. We refer to this selection and relationship between user/target as audience; it identifies who will be contacted. The relationship will have an N to N cardinality, which means that a user will be related to several stores and vice versa.
It will be necessary to filter the selected audience, leaving only the cases we are interested in contacting, and discarding notifications already sent to the pair, or any particular communication logic that is being developed. Once we have cleared the pairs and obtained only those that are of our interest, it is time to select the best candidate for communication; we refer to this step as a score. At this point, the N to N relationship must be deduplicated, and only one store must be selected for each user. This step is designed so that any type of scoring can be simply included, whether it is a heuristic or a score resulting from a machine learning model.
These components are inherent to each strategy; that is to say that each of them may have its special features, and the result will be a set of pairs where the user will be unique.
Up to this point, we are talking about atomized communications, something we are not looking forward to and that, in our view, needs a further development stage. . That is why after getting all the communications, a general de-duplication process is carried out across strategies, finally selecting the best one for each user. We refer to this step as conciliation, for which any type of scoring is also valid, from a heuristic to a particular model.
Once the best communication strategy has been decided on, we move on to the notification process; in our case, it will be by means of a push notification to the mobile phone, taking into account the optimum time of contact for each store to finally trigger the push.
In this last step, the dynamic allocation of communication content is carried out, according to the strategy in question. This is a critical part of the process as any error could potentially unleash thousands of notifications.
Strategies
Market basket
One of the strategies developed is based on an association rules algorithm. The main objective of this initiative is to find a consequence relationship among the different items (Merchant Category Code — MCC from now on) where it is possible to make payments. In order to do so, we have analyzed the payments of a given period of time looking for patterns among those made by the same user in the lapse of two hours.

From ON to OFF
Within the ecosystem of Mercado Libre, we have both the marketplace universe and the physical world of in-store payments. The aim of this initiative is to achieve traceability between the interests of users in both fields. We want a user to know that, if for some reason, he did not make a purchase in the marketplace, he can get the same product in a physical store using Mercado Pago under the same conditions and many times even at a discount.
In order to carry out this initiative, we first defined the items, since not all of them had their own correlation within the marketplace. Then, we identified all the users who showed interest in products from the selected categories and had not made the purchases. Once we had both the stores and the users, we only took those connections that were at a certain distance from what we considered the frequent location. We could only establish one frequent location since we only had the geolocation at the times that the user agreed to share that information with us. That is why the selection was an estimate of what we considered would be the best for them.
This is how we get personalized recommendations based on the user’s interests, such as the items intensively searched for on the site, while keeping the sale within our ecosystem and showing other purchasing options.
To measure the contact opportunity we would have, we chose some MCCs as a model, calculated how many users were interested in some of the products in their category and then found how many users could be offered a store near their location.
From this analysis, we can conclude that an offer within a distance of 2 km would reach a little less than half of the interested users (41.33%). It should be pointed out that, in practice, the distance we cover in our search of the offer varies according to the category in question.

Results
To compare results, we measured our strategies against one of the strategies that was being developed with the previous methodology. In the graph, you can see it under the name “Favorite”. The metric used to calculate effectiveness was open rate, taken as the number of notifications opened over the number of notifications shown.
The graphs show that the initiatives implemented with the audience generation model yielded better results; this can be seen both in the daily opening and in the accumulated data.
The best comparison is between “Favorites” and “Similar to Favorites”, since both strategies are similar and they seek to contact the user by offering him the stores of those categories that we consider are their favorite. The result was undoubtedly better than in the ad hoc initiative — in some cases even doubling the percentage.
The fact that the process is scalable gives us enough flexibility to add new strategies or prioritize according to a particular action, such as discounts targeted to a certain group of users and discounts on a particular item, among others. Moreover, thanks to the fact that candidate communications compete with one another and that we select the most appropriate one for the user, we ensure that the channel is not saturated and that we only communicate through the one we believe will attract the user’s attention. In this way, we achieve greater interest and avoid audience exhaustion.
We are now focusing on orchestrating and prioritizing communications, but we still have a long way to go. We are to keep focusing our efforts on communicating effectively with a smaller universe of users. But such contact must be valuable, provide a service without generating annoyance and prevent users from getting used to irrelevant notifications.
Those who claim that less is more, were certainly on the right track.
