From blank page to perfect pitch: how AI is transforming ads at leboncoin
By Julia Langlois, Product Manager at leboncoin
Writing the perfect ad is an art form — one that requires finding just the right words to capture attention, build trust, and drive action. But for many of our users, this crucial step often feels like a daunting challenge. Some find themselves staring at a blank screen, sometimes even browsing other ads for inspiration — we call it the “blank page syndrome.” Others, discouraged by the writing process, opt for minimal descriptions that barely describe their items. This creates a vicious cycle: poorly described items don’t sell well, leading to frustrated sellers who become less likely to post future ads on our platform, ultimately affecting both the quality and quantity of ads on leboncoin.
We could have stuck to the basics — offering writing tips or template descriptions. But in a world where AI is transforming how we work, why not dream bigger? The challenge wasn’t just about fixing the “blank page syndrome” — it was about rethinking the entire ad creation experience. This led us to develop a generative AI-powered feature that can change how our users create ads — automatic ad description generation. With just a few clicks, users can now generate relevant, compelling descriptions. It’s a game-changer for those looking to save time and boost their success on our platform!
Building an AI Co-pilot, not a user replacement
Picture this: you’re about to list your Just Dance Wii game for sale. You’ve snapped photos of the disc and cover (both in surprisingly good condition, considering the number of living room dance parties it’s survived), but when it comes to writing the description, you pause. Should you mention it’s the 2012 edition? List all the hit songs? Talk about those memorable family game nights? This is exactly where our AI steps in to help.
Our new feature acts like a smart writing partner. Upload your photos, fill in a few basic details about your item, and watch as AI crafts a description that captures everything important. Available across several categories — from consumer goods to vehicles — it’s like having an expert seller by your side, suggesting the perfect words to showcase your item.
But here’s the key: we’re not replacing the human touch. The AI-generated description is just a starting point. Sellers maintain full control: they can tweak the text, add personal details, or start fresh if they prefer. Some use it as is, others add their personal flair — and that’s exactly how we designed it. This flexibility ensures we maintain the authentic, human element that makes our classifieds platform unique.
Under the hood, we’re powered by Claude Haiku and AWS Bedrock infrastructure, but what matters is less the technology — it’s how seamlessly it fits into the ad creation process. The algorithm considers your photos, title, and item details to generate relevant, structured descriptions that feel natural and engaging.
The Journey: from concept to reality
When my UX partner, Nafi Cissé and I pitched this idea internally, we knew we were onto something exciting. But we also knew we had to get it just right. You see, leboncoin isn’t just any classifieds platform — it has its own unique language.
Our users write concise, to-the-point ads that get straight to what matters. It’s not about flowery prose; it’s about using the right keywords in both the title and description to ensure your item shows up in searches. More importantly, it’s about sounding natural, like a real person selling their stuff to another real person.
This deep understanding of our platform’s DNA guided our entire approach. During user testing, our first attempts at AI-generated descriptions fell flat — they were too verbose, too formal, reading more like product catalogs than leboncoin’s ads. “Where’s the human touch?” we heard.
That feedback was gold. Working with Nafi, we conducted multiple rounds of interviews, testing different versions of our AI outputs. Each iteration brought us closer to that perfect leboncoin tone — concise yet complete, informative yet conversational. Our users became our co-creators, helping us fine-tune not just the length and style of descriptions, but also the essential keywords that make ads more discoverable.

You’re probably wondering if certain aspects of the discovery phase evolve when creating an AI product, and as a true Norman, which formulate ambiguous responses, I’d say, “it depends.” The traditional discovery process remains essential, but with AI products, we found ourselves cycling through iterations much faster, constantly balancing technical capabilities with user expectations.
What’s particularly noteworthy is how much of our discovery process ultimately centered around prompt creation. The prompts became the critical interface between user needs and generative AI capabilities — what we were really testing and iterating on were the results that flowed from these prompts. This is where we invested significant iteration cycles, fine-tuning to achieve the right balance of guidance and freedom.
The key difference was not just focusing on what the AI could do, but on when it should step back and let human creativity shine through.
Smart AI: less is more
When you’re excited about a new AI feature, it’s tempting to go all out. Why not let users generate multiple versions? Why not offer endless refinements? But here’s the thing: sometimes constraints lead to better results.
We made a deliberate choice to limit users to one AI-generated description per ad. It’s not just about managing costs (though yes, AI is expensive) or reducing our environmental footprint (AI models are notorious energy consumers). It’s about encouraging users to think carefully about their ads from the start.
Breaking the ordinary: when legal becomes part of the design team
Here’s something you don’t hear every day: our legal team was actually excited about an AI project. Usually, legal reviews come at the end of the design process — you know the drill: you’ve built something cool, and then legal comes in with a list of changes that send you back to the drawing board. But we flipped the script.
Instead of the traditional “build first, legal later” approach, we invited our legal team to join the design process from day one. They weren’t just reviewers; they became active collaborators, helping us shape the feature while ensuring compliance. Together, we tackled the tricky questions early: How do we handle user data with our AI partner? Where and how do we tell users that AI is helping write their descriptions?
This collaborative approach paid off. No last-minute redesigns, no painful compromises. Just smooth sailing from design to deployment. It turns out that when legal teams help build the boat, they’re less likely to sink it later.
Results and future horizons
What started as our idea quickly turned into something our users genuinely loved. Our automatic description feature has won over users with its simplicity, but the real story lies in the numbers: ads using this feature are seeing a 20% increase in both inquiries and completed transactions.”This is exactly what I needed,” became a common refrain in our testing sessions. The feature wasn’t just saving time; it was helping users create better, more effective ads.
This commitment to innovation hasn’t gone unnoticed. In March 2025, leboncoin was awarded the grand prize for innovation at the Grand Prix Favor’i e-commerce, organized by FEVAD. This recognition highlights our ability to anticipate users’ needs and provide them with cutting-edge solutions. It validates our ongoing efforts to enhance the selling experience and reinforces our mission to make online transactions simpler, faster, and more efficient.
Having deployed this functionality in the mobility market, we’re taking this feature into exciting new territory — real estate ads. But hold on: why not just roll out what we already have? Well… selling an apartment is quite different from selling a Just Dance game.
While our discovery process will be faster this time around, we’re still asking the fundamental questions: What makes a compelling real estate ad? Which details matter most to potential buyers? The answers might surprise us. Beyond the basics like square footage and number of rooms, we might want to incorporate neighborhood data and local points of interest. After all, people aren’t just buying four walls — they’re buying into a lifestyle, a community, a morning commute to their favorite boulangerie (yes — leboncoin operates in France!).
This expansion represents more than just adding a new category — it’s about understanding how different items tell different stories. Each category on leboncoin has its own language, its own priorities, and its own measure of what makes an ad effective. That’s why we’ll keep learning, keep asking questions, and keep refining our approach with each new implementation.
