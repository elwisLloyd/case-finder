# Matching LinkedIn members with the right Premium products

Источник: https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/machine-learning/matching-linkedin-members-with-the-right-premium-products

LinkedIn Premium is designed to help professionals, job seekers, and small businesses stand out, connect with others more effectively, and gain insights that can help in career development or business growth. Premium has four products (seen below in Figure 1). With each product having its own distinct features and pricing, effectively conveying the unique value of the products to a broad audience is crucial.
Matching the ideal offering to each member is an intricate challenge that relies on understanding what a member needs and communicating the value of the offerings to them. When done successfully, members can more easily achieve their goals and find more value on LinkedIn.
As seen in Figure 2 below, there are various channels to communicate the value of Premium, from who viewed your profile (left), to the job search (center) to homepage (right). Communicating at the wrong time, at the wrong place, or with unsuitable messages, risks creating a nuisance to members and losing the opportunity to truly help them find value.
To solve this problem, we developed a unified machine learning platform capable of holistic optimization of member value by orchestrating:
- Which product to recommend
- What creative (design elements) should be used to communicate the matching value proposition
- Which channel to show the creative
- Whether or not show these messages at all, while intelligently adapting to member feedback in a near real-time manner
As part of our acceleration and commitment to bring the right product offering to our members, the Premium engineering team needed to enhance the system to deliver value in a more timely manner. In this blog, we will highlight how the platform improves our experimentation capabilities as well as bringing positive impact for members.

Offering the right value proposition is largely done through a multi-modal creative which consists of text, promotions, and a call to action button. To tackle this matching problem, our platform leverages a combination of member & content understanding, deep matching, and an explore/exploit layer. We also devised an architecture for near-real time offline training and real time inference to balance model performance and online infrastructure requirements.
ML Platform
The objective of the platform is to match the right arm to a member. An arm, consists of a triplet of the following:
- Product - which of the four online products we offer
- Creative - which messaging and format of the creative that will be shown
- Channel - which channel to reach out on (mobile, desktop, homepage, etc.) 
In addition, for each channel there is an additional special cooloff arm representing when/if to stop showing, if it is not the right time or to prevent fatigue. The cardinality for the set of arms can potentially be in the thousands or more. Sourcing and matching in realtime can thus be a challenge. To help make this possible, we pre-compute embeddings offline for each arm and member to be used for scoring online. An arm embedding is a numerical vector representation of a multi-modal creative incorporating text, channel and product information. Member embedding transforms the characteristics and interactions of users into a compact numerical representation enabling more efficient user-based recommendations and behavior predictions.
Construction of candidate arms
To generate arms, we first list all the possible combinations of products and channels, which is a set number. Then we add in the creative, which can consist of the content as well as different design formats. We collaborate with the marketing team to construct these combinations, ensuring each creative properly represents premium value propositions that will resonate with members.
Scoring for a (member, arm) pair
Seen in Figure 3, our neural network model consists of three major components: member & content understanding, deep matching, and the explore/exploit layer.
Member & content understanding
To score arms, we consolidate and learn representations of the member and arms (called embeddings). The member tower (top right of Figure 3) consolidates the following raw member data and learns a consolidated vector representation:
- Member Profile Data - Self-inputted data and profile information, such as their industry, positions, skills, etc. This could be standardized categorical features or raw text from their profile
- Member Activity Data - Member activities on the site, such as applying to jobs, viewing profiles, or interacting with posts on the Feed
- Member Derived Data - These include AI model inferences built by our internal teams, such as intent prediction scores or general member embeddings
- Context - What time of day they are on LinkedIn, on what type of device
The arm tower (top left of Figure 3) consists of
- Creative Raw Text: We process the raw text of the content using either a pre-trained LLM embedding or a fine-tuned model
- Product One-Hot: A one-hot encoding of which product the creative corresponds to
- Channel One-Hot: A one-hot encoding of which channel the creative associated with
We’ll see later that this two-tower architecture allows us to pre-compute member and arm embeddings independently, which reduces latency and compute-resources at inference time.
Deep matching
Once the member and arm embeddings are computed from the model, we feed them through several standard dense layers, and have a final binary output to predict whether the member will interact with the arm (e.g., clicked on or converted after seeing the content). This training task brings the member and arm embeddings closer together, ensuring that similar (member, arm) pairs (those likely to interact) are grouped near each other. By doing so, we reduce errors and refine the model’s ability to make accurate predictions.
Explore/exploit layer
Utilizing only the above, we would just have a standard ranking model, where we “greedily” always select the arm for a member with the highest score. In reinforcement learning (RL), we need to continuously collect rewards and information to have better estimates of the value from a (member, arm) pair. This means, instead of choosing the optimal arm every time, we may decide to explore a different arm for the sake of collecting information and improving the models’ understanding of other arms that aren’t chosen as often.
Note the “explore/exploit” layer at the end of the process in Figure 3. This ensures that the model does not always return a deterministic output on the same input values, enabling the exploration behavior above. We employ a variant of Neural Thompson Sampling. We use an approximation which only uses the last layer’s weights to compute the posterior, which speeds up training. In practice, our version can be computationally efficient and in specific cases more accurate.
Once a member needs to be shown an impression, we can score this model in parallel on all available arms, and return the arm to show as the highest sampled score. This builds in exploration naturally, and the exploration decreases over time as the variance decreases with more training data.
System design
We’ll now discuss how the overall system looks integrating online inference and the feedback loop (the current system does batch offline inference for initial proof of concept, but this will eventually be moved to online).
The model is currently retrained offline in batches every few hours. The training data consists of each row having the impression data (including the arm shown to the user), and the labels being binary: whether they interacted with the impression or not. During initialization, the model collects data by serving arms randomly. We then iteratively retrain the model, collecting new evaluation data and comparing the previous model to the current one on the new data. If the performance is better (or neutral), we can deploy the fresher model to replace the current one in production. This process is completely automated and more streamlined than traditional A/B testing, where updates are delayed for weeks and require separate, ad-hoc analysis to identify receptive audiences if the new content does not perform well.
For the online service, we have a store of all the arms/contents available, which we check every time before scoring. This may change at any point if we introduce content that reflects new product values for example. To improve efficiency, our model doesn’t need to be retrained for new arms - rather, it utilizes the arm’s attributes to generalize immediately. We score all candidate arms in parallel for a member, and return the highest scoring arm ranked by objective function described in the above section. Recall that the scores are not deterministic on fixed inputs due to the explore/exploit layer, so we can and will explore new arms live. Member rewards are also considered when we rank all eligible arms.
Results
With the streamlined system, we launched four experiments with a single layer for explore-exploit in just two months. We tested hundreds of creatives across multiple channels and increased our experiment velocity by 10x. These creative tests would not have been possible over this timeframe in the past, and would have involved lots of manual efforts to provide personalized experience.
The experiments also have statistically significant improvement on Premium true north metrics: +0.7% on platform conversion rate without negatively impacting member retention. We’re also testing the model structure proposed in the earlier section against this baseline, and expecting to see better results with improved personalization.
Future directions
In its initial deployments, our platform has improved the value members receive from LinkedIn. However, there are still opportunities to expand this system.
Looking ahead, we’re imagining a scenario where we accompany a member throughout their professional journey. For instance, a member who initially uses LinkedIn for job searching might, upon securing a job in sales, might benefit from our sales-oriented products and resources.
This shift requires a more sophisticated approach than currently employed, suggesting a move towards deep reinforcement learning models, like Deep Q-Networks, instead of solely relying on bandit algorithms. Such advanced models would enable us to optimize the member experience over a longer horizon, ensuring that our value proposition remains relevant and impactful throughout the various stages of their career.
Simultaneously, manually creating combinations of value propositions is not scalable and fails to deliver completely personalized solutions. Integrating with generative AI would be essential to streamline this process. By adopting GAI and deeper, more nuanced learning models, we could significantly enhance the long-term value delivered to each member, fostering a more profound and enduring relationship with our platform.
