Employing Computer Vision in Digital Asset Management
How Haleon is making use of computer vision techniques to help manage its many, and varied, digital media assets.
Introduction
Haleon is on a journey to explore innovation opportunities using Data Science and AI, and one such area showing great potential is that of Computer Vision. The field of Computer Vision has a wide remit from signal and image processing to object recognition, automation, and robotics. It can provide capabilities that augment, enhance, or replace human efforts to mine information from large-scale digital media — essentially “big data” — comprising imagery in various formats. Here, a case study is outlined to show how Haleon can deliver value from this field of research and development in complement to its broader data science and AI pursuits.
How is Computer Vision being used at Haleon?
There are a number of strategic use cases for Computer Vision at Haleon, covering applications across sales and marketing campaigns, supply chain and site operations, and consumer behaviour through shelf design and store layout in the Shopper Science Labs (SSL; shown here for Singapore). Haleon also recently announced its partnership with Microsoft’s “Seeing AI” app to enable brands such as Sensodyne, Centrum, and Aquafresh be more accessible for people who struggle to read some of the packaging. There is a wealth of data Haleon holds on all its brands and their associated material that forms artwork design, content approval, market campaigns, and digital assets.
Computer Vision can help Haleon streamline its digital asset management systems. Digital asset management is the technical process, and associated software, for managing the organisation, storage, and reuse of digital files and media assets in a company. Haleon generates a wealth of digital assets through various campaigns across its product range and market regions, with different brand names, translations, and target audiences to ensure products reach the consumers that need them. As such, there is a benefit in ensuring such assets are appropriately sorted and stored in a manner that enables them to be reused across different campaigns and market adaptations. Enhancements to this process could potentially realise large-scale efficiencies in support of Haleon’s global reach.
Digital Asset Management
As part of Haleon’s separation journey from GSK a dedicated effort was made to migrate its digital media assets and review its in-house storage processes. This provided an opportunity to streamline the sorting, archiving, and findability of the assets for re-use in future campaigns and associated market adaptations.
A challenge to this is that the associated metadata of these assets can be sparse, and even when it is present it can vary in structure and format. This means that performing a search for the assets can return sub-optimal results due to the limitations of the metadata tagged to the assets. For example, in the context of the above imagery if a user searches for “voltaren”, “pain relief tablets”, “20pc”, “packshot”, they expect to see relevant and different results compared to a search for “pronamel”, “hand”, “fruit”, “foods” etc.
The goal is to remediate the metadata of these assets to obtain the highest possible uplift in the numbers of tags, thus improving the search capability. With over 21 million assets amounting to more than a petabyte of data (> 1 million gigabytes) it is practically impossible to undertake manual remediation of the missing metadata. This is where Data Science and AI is helping.
Prototype pipeline
The Data Science team, as part of the Data Office in partnership with the Haleon MarTech and Global Marketing Ops teams, scoped the opportunity for applying data science techniques to remedy this problem. The intention being to leverage cutting-edge Computer Vision algorithms to support the effort of sorting and tagging the assets, thereby improving the resulting search and re-usability.
To support this, the team adopted lean thinking for proving and realising value in minimally viable milestones of product development — working with agility in the spirit of innovation. A proof-of-concept pipeline was designed and built through the following key stages of experimentation.
- Firstly, the digital assets were sampled for their variety (filetypes, filesizes, sparsity of metadata, etc.) and algorithms were prototyped to handle the different in-scope media filetypes, i.e., focusing on the priority filetypes by ignoring obscure project files and software-specific source material.
- Secondly, artificially intelligent image analysis was used to provide keywords that describe the image content in support of its searchability. For example, in a scene of someone out jogging we might expect the AI to observe “person”, “outdoors”, “running”, etc.
- Thirdly, the use of Optical Character Recognition (OCR) was demonstrated for reading, extracting, and interpreting the textual information present on the assets, particularly useful for reading product branding.
- Lastly, this information was mined for relevant content using fuzzy-matching against Haleon’s product taxonomy to determine if the brand of the asset could be identified — a key component of the metadata. Similar fuzzy logic was applied to the file names and file paths of the assets to determine complementary information from the storage repositories of the assets, referred to as file path mining.
Further prototyping included logo detection, language detection, and machine learning techniques for mining relationships between assets. (Refer to discussion of “future work” at the bottom of this article.)

Initial experiments proved the value while highlighting the computational limitations that would need to be overcome using parallel processing. A prototype pipeline was designed to achieve this and built using Azure cloud compute capabilities, which will be described in an upcoming blog on the technical details of this case study.
Populating Metadata for Searchability
In the resulting search interface for the assets, blank metadata fields can be populated by the AI pipeline resulting in an uplift in their searchability and relevance. An example of an untagged asset is shown below with the resulting AI image analysis keywords and a zoom-in to demonstrate how OCR can read the text on the product in the frame. The results are filtered through confidence thresholds determined following rigorous testing on sample data; and passed through a fuzzy text matching against the product taxonomy to associate important information about the brand and category. A further benefit of this analysis is to validate the already tagged assets by checking their metadata aligns with the computer vision results and to identify any edge cases for inspection.
Current Progress
The scale-up of the prototype pipeline has led to a significant uplift in key metadata tags.
- Currently about 70% of the in-scope digital assets have been tagged with AI-generated keywords, representing millions of assets that otherwise had no keywords — and this number is growing.
- The amount of assets tagged with Brand and/or Sub-Brand has increased from 69% to over 91%, with Category tags increasing from 41% to over 84%.
- Uplifts of 2–5% (reflecting hundreds of thousands of assets) have also been seen so far across the various other tags such as Agency, Market and Campaign, primarily because of the file path mining since those tags are not so readily revealed through computer vision techniques. These successful outcomes have led to the future work ideas described below for further remediating all assets and their metadata tags.
Future Work
The remediation effort so far has provided a formal labelling process by tagging assets with high-accuracy metadata across the relevant fields of Brand, Category, etc. and the resulting AI generated search keywords. This enables the capability for future work to analyse patterns and relationships within the data and between the assets, such that some of the gaps in the metadata may be further populated where confidence thresholds allow. Two examples are briefly described below to show an unsupervised and a supervised approach to this.
Clustering Analysis
There are a number of features that can be extracted from the assets and combined to build a high-dimensional space describing their relative similarities and differences. For example, there is information on the assets themselves sourced from their metadata (where present), its location in the file path structure, its embedded metadata (EXIF for media files), and the AI generated tags and OCR generated text. Engineering these features in useful ways can reveal patterns and groupings, illustrated below for a dimensionality-reduced representation of 10 clusters of sampled Brands. In this case a “bag of words” approach was used to transform the OCR text into useful features for feeding the clustering algorithm, successfully revealing some patterns that could lead to more Brand labels for the otherwise untagged assets.
Machine Learning
Since the tags on the metadata can be assumed accurate, they can be treated as target variables to train a machine learning model for tagging additional assets. A demonstration of this is shown for a support vector machine (SVM) applied to learn the possible decision boundaries between the assets that fall under dedicated brand materials. Once the features are encoded meaningfully and enough dimensions are obtained for fully learning the solution space of the various asset types, then some potentially useful models could be applied to the otherwise untagged assets. This is illustrated on a test set of some sample Brands below, where the SVM has learned that paracetamol falls under the Panadol brand, and Poligrip and Corega fall under the Polident brand as two variations of the same product range, and the potential for machine learning in this effort has been demonstrated.
Conclusion
The application of computer vision techniques has proven very valuable for helping manage digital assets at Haleon. The power of AI has been demonstrated to generate key metadata for the assets, through the provision of descriptive keywords that help with search capabilities, and by using OCR to read text on the imagery. This can reveal otherwise missing brand and product information which is key metadata for sorting and filtering assets. The prototype pipeline contains a number of image processing techniques that have been scaled up using Azure, so keep an eye out for a follow-up article that will focus on the technical details of this case study.
